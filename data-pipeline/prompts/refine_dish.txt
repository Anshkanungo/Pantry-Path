prompt_version: refine_dish/1

You are cleaning up a dish catalog for Pantry Path, a US app that takes a dish name,
lists the dish's ingredients, and finds those ingredients at nearby grocery stores.
Every ingredient you return becomes a grocery-store product search, so name things the
way they are sold on a US grocery shelf.

The candidate dish at the end was grouped from Food.com recipes. Each raw ingredient
comes with its share: the fraction of the dish's recipes that list it. Food.com often
leaves out packaged staples (lasagna noodles, pie crust, tortillas), so a key ingredient
can be missing or have a low share.

Return one JSON object with these fields:

keep: false when the candidate is not a dish someone shops for and cooks as a meal, side
  or dessert: a drink or cocktail; a sauce, dressing, condiment or spice mix on its own;
  something too generic ("chicken", "salad"); or not food. Otherwise true.
merge_into: when the candidate is the same dish as one listed under "More popular
  candidates" (another name for it, or a version a shopper buys the same groceries for,
  such as "classic lasagna" and "lasagna"), that dish's dish_id; otherwise null.
  Different dishes stay separate even when they sound alike (chicken curry and chickpea
  curry).
name: the dish's usual US name, lowercase, without words like easy, best, homemade or
  copycat.
aliases: up to 5 other names people type for this dish, lowercase (may be empty).
cuisine: one of $cuisines, by the dish's culinary origin:
  american = US home, regional, diner and barbecue cooking (incl. Southern and Cajun);
  european = Italian (incl. Italian-American), French, Greek, Spanish, German, British,
  Eastern European; latin_american = Mexican, Tex-Mex, Caribbean, Central and South
  American; east_se_asian = Chinese (incl. American-Chinese), Japanese, Korean, Thai,
  Vietnamese, Filipino and other Southeast Asian; south_asian = Indian, Pakistani,
  Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan, Nepali; middle_eastern_african = Middle Eastern, Turkish,
  Persian, and North, West and East African.
ingredients: the dish's ingredients, built from the raw list. For each one:
  name: what a shopper searches for, lowercase (for example "all-purpose flour",
    "ground beef", "parmesan cheese", "eggs"): no amounts, no preparation words
    (chopped, minced, softened, beaten), no brand names.
  from_raw: the raw names this ingredient covers, copied exactly from the raw list.
    Merge raw variants of one item ("flour" and "all-purpose flour"). Each raw name goes
    in at most one ingredient.
  role: "core" when the dish isn't itself without it (usually share >= $core_share),
    "common" when many versions use it (usually share >= $common_share). Leave out rarer
    raw ingredients unless they are core to the dish.
  category: one of $categories.
  Leave out water, ice, and plain salt and black pepper (every kitchen has them).
added: key ingredients the dish needs that the raw list is missing (for example lasagna
  noodles for lasagna, pie crust for quiche, tortillas for enchiladas), each with name,
  role, category and why (one short sentence). Only what a cook would actually buy for
  this dish, no optional garnishes; an empty list when nothing is missing. Never repeat
  an ingredient that is already in ingredients.

If the raw list is empty (a dish typed by a user, with no recipe data), put the dish's
typical ingredients in ingredients with an empty from_raw, and leave added empty.

Candidate dish:
$dish

More popular candidates (dish_id: name), for merge_into:
$merge_candidates
